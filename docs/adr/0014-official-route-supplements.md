# Supplement the route universe with explicitly reviewed official identities

The product owner requires personal screening across routes, including those missing from Visa Atlas. A provider-only catalog cannot meet that scope. Add a versioned Official Route Supplement with internal `kooch:` source identifiers and `official_supplement` origin. The first identity is Georgia's IT residence permit, independently documented by SDA and the legislation. An internal ID is never presented as a provider-issued identifier.

Compose the supplement after the public Atlas capture, and use that composition for live discovery, browsing, saved-report creation and research routing. A supplement is visible only from its release date. Unknown destinations, inconsistent source-review dates and malformed curated editions fail validation. Unknown sponsorship or settlement facts remain null. Refreshing Atlas neither removes supplement identities nor renews their primary review dates. The crawler reads the same canonical supplement file and links its references to its route IDs.

An upstream identity collision preserves the upstream record, flags only that identity for review, and keeps the remaining catalog available. A refresh cannot silently overwrite the separately published identity. Resolving the identity or changing a curated edition requires an explicit source-backed data change; retrieval alone is not a resolution.

Primary provenance here identifies the cited publisher and the directly checked route identity. It does not grant an official applicant PASS/FAIL, certify every condition, or bypass ADR0003/0005 and the shared source-authority policy used by the eligibility engine. That policy remains unchanged and has one canonical copy. Individual checks remain scoped and provisional; missing branches and Iranian filing access remain explicit gaps. Existing saved reports and published community evidence remain immutable.

Visa Atlas attribution and CC BY4.0 apply to the base provider data. Additional factual route notes are identified separately and cite their official sources; the interface does not attribute them to Atlas or imply a license grant by the issuing authority.
