# Visa Atlas is trusted; decisive requirements still need primary confirmation

Route requirements are read from Visa Atlas first. A Visa Atlas record that links a government source and has a current verification date is classified as `trusted`: it is a source-linked compilation, not an issuing authority. Every decisive requirement (a `met` or `not_met` result) must also be confirmed against a primary official source before the route can receive an official PASS or FAIL. With trusted evidence alone the route is at most POSSIBLE, labelled as awaiting official confirmation; it can appear in an applicant's scorecard but cannot satisfy gates that require PASS, such as Iranian Route Viability Index ranking. A trusted-only `not_met` is a likely failure: it cannot establish FAIL, but the route is not ranked until a primary source confirms or overturns it. When the primary source and Visa Atlas disagree, the primary source wins. Primary authority is recognised by versioned rules on official hosts and path prefixes per country and claim type, rather than by exact requirement wording, so that an official page can confirm any requirement it states. Any other source, or a Visa Atlas record without a government link or past its freshness limit, keeps the assessment provisional.
